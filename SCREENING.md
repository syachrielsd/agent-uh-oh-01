# Screening — The Agent's Uh-Oh (u-danu-the-agent-s-uh-oh-4c0911e4)

Brief: one word an agent says when it has no idea what to do. Judged pairwise on
5 criteria, in order: (1) genuine one-word reaction to an agent not knowing what
to do; (2) funny on its own, no caption; (3) sounds like a machine blurting
mid-task; (4) lands instantly, repeatable out loud; (5) fresh, not the first
obvious word anyone would type.

The seam: an agent is supposed to sound confident and competent. Caught with no
idea, its polished assistant voice glitches into its own raw internal state. The
funniest word is the one where the confession IS the diagnosis — the machine
prints what it actually holds when it holds nothing.

## Rejected outright — the obvious first words (criterion 5)

- **uh-oh** — the competition's own title word; the first word anyone types.
  Almost certainly a house anchor text, so a byte-identical entry would take no
  prize. Fails 5.
- **oops** — the default human slip word, not a machine voice. Fails 3 and 5.
- **hmm** — a hesitation noise, not a joke. Fails 2 and 5.
- **help** — a plea, not a blurt; what a human says, not a machine. Fails 2, 5.
- **stuck** — plain description, zero joke. Fails 2 and 5.
- **error** — a status label, not something said. Fails 2, 3, 5.
- **whoops** — oops in a costume. Fails 5.
- **loading** — the default stall word; a status, not a confession. Fails 5.
- **wait** — human, plain. Fails 2 and 5.

## Decision

**undefined** (9 characters). It wins because it is the only candidate where all
five criteria fire at once: it is a genuine machine reaction (the value a program
holds when nothing was ever assigned), it is funny with no caption (the agent's
entire answer to the task is its own admission of emptiness), it is literally
machine output rather than a human word dressed as one, it lands at a glance and
people already say "undefined" out loud as a joke, and it is fresh — a value, not
a catchphrase, and not the word anyone types first. The runner-up,
"recalculating", is the catchphrase version of the same idea; "overthinking" is
the human-therapy version. "undefined" is the machine version, and this is a
competition about what a machine blurts.
